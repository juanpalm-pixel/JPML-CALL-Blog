What is Le(T)TAT?

March 16th 2026 Hilary Term

When we build digital tools for language learning, it’s easy to get distracted by the "shiny" factor --the newest AI, the smoothest graphics, or the most complex game mechanics--. But how do we ensure these tools actually help someone learn a language?

At Trinity College Dublin, the Le(T)TAT Framework (formerly known as TATL) (developed by our very own Neasa Ní Chiaráin) provides a blueprint for answering that question. It moves us away from focusing solely on the "computer" and toward a unified, interdisciplinary approach to development.

What is Le(T)TAT?

The Le(T)TAT framework is an initial analysis tool designed to help developers and practitioners evaluate or plan game-based CALL (Computer-Assisted Language Learning) platforms. The acronym stands for:

  • Learner (and Teacher)
  • Theory
  • Action
  • Technology

1. The Learner... for whom?

We often treat "the user" as a monolith, but Le(T)TAT reminds us that learning happens in a complex environment. To design a tool that works, we must consider:

  • Existing Proficiency: Is the level appropriate?
  • Cognitive Style: How does the individual process information?
  • Sociocultural Background: Does the content resonate with their reality?
  • Willingness to Engage: Are they motivated to use this specific technology to acquire the target language?

2. Theory

Theory is what helps us know if what we’re doing treads water, i.e., if what we are creating can actually be impactful, or simply another toy. In CALL, common theories include Social Constructivism, Sociocultural Theory, and Interactionist SLA.

Key Insight: The choice of activity should always be rooted in a theory of language acquisition. For decades, the dominant approach has been Communicative, focusing on functional, meaningful communication.
