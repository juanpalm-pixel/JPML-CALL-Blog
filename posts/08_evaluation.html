Evaluating CALL Applications

March 23rd 2026 Hilary Term

Evaluation is a systematic process designed to determine the value, worth, or merit of a CALL activity. While often understudied in the field, a sound evaluation ensures that the technology we develop is actually "fit for purpose". Within the LeTAT Framework (see the previous blog entry), evaluation is split into two critical stages: Usability and Theory.

Evaluating Usability

Usability focuses on how effective and satisfying a tool is for the learner. This is typically measured through frameworks like the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) (which is still used today), which examines "Behavioural Intention", "Actual Use", "Perceived Usefulness" and "Perceived Ease of Use" (read more). More complex tools may use the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) to account for social influence and facilitating conditions (also see here).

Evaluating Theory

Here we see if the application actually results in beneficial learning outcomes, i.e., we move from "does it work?" to "does it teach?". The most reliable way to evaluate theory is through a controlled participant experiment. To do that, we use the following:

  • Random Sampling: Choosing a sample from the population and splitting it into two groups.
  • Treatment Group: Uses the CALL application being tested.
  • Control Group: Does not use the application, perhaps using an alternative learning method instead.
  • Comparison: Compare the performance of both groups after a set period to observe the effect of the application.

Comparing the Two Approaches

Deciding which evaluation to run often comes down to available resources and project goals.

Feature Evaluating Usability Evaluating Theory
Effort Relatively quick and easy. Highly resource-intensive.
User Interaction Attempt tasks and fill in one questionnaire. Frequent use over a prolonged period.
Assessment Focuses on user satisfaction. Requires pre-, post-, and delayed post-tests.

Summary

Ultimately, a successful CALL application must bridge the gap between technical innovation and pedagogical effectiveness. By combining quick usability checks with rigorous theoretical evaluation, we ensure that our digital tools provide a meaningful path to language acquisition.
